# Walkthrough script — target 1 minute 50 seconds

This is a recording script, not a recorded video. Rehearse once and keep the exported video below two minutes.

**0:00–0:15 — The verdict.**

“The general manager wants to replace the ten lowest-earning machines. Our dashboard asks: are they bad machines, or did they have fewer opportunities to earn? Our answer is to pause blanket replacement and spend just $8,700 testing the evidence.”

**0:15–0:35 — Floor explorer.**

“Change the dates and drill into Back Forty. Compare theoretical earnings per available hour, not total cash alone. The full investigation separates exposure, game mix, location, machine performance and payout variation. Nine of the manager's ten spent time here. One machine, M-1158, warrants inspection after screening all 310.”

**0:35–0:55 — Demand and downtime.**

“Back Forty weakened during a documented walkway closure and improved after reopening. That supports an access hypothesis, not a proven signage effect. Downtime opportunity is modeled missing play; guests might simply have moved to another machine.”

**0:55–1:10 — Guests and comps.**

“Choose a guest group. These are tracked players only, aggregated across complete months. The top one percent accounts for twenty percent of tracked wagers. Comp costs are counted once. Redemption point prices fell in May, even within the same benefit and tier.”

**1:10–1:30 — Model lab.**

“The weekday forecast was frozen before May and June. Its error was about $69,000 per day, versus $218,000 for the baseline. The uncertainty band missed its nominal coverage target, which we disclose. This supports scheduling routine maintenance on quieter Tuesdays.”

**1:30–1:50 — Action studio and evidence.**

“Fund a $1,200 inspection and a $7,500 wayfinding test, retaining $91,300. Move the displacement assumption to see how signage's break-even requirement changes. Every action links to its source, alternative explanation and limitation. We buy information and measure results before committing the remaining budget.”
